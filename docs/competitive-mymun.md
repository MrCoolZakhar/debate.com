# Competitive brief: mymun vs Gavelling

## The read

mymun is winning the screens where an organiser spends hours, and Gavelling is winning almost everywhere else. Their applications To Do queue — one unified list of chairs, delegates and whole delegations, two always-visible segmented controls ("To accept | To assign | Any" crossed with "Paid | Unpaid | Any"), a "To Do 39" count badge in the sidebar, payment fractions on the row, and Accept/Reject as half-row-width targets — is designed for 991 repetitions, while Gavelling's is designed for one excellent decision. They also have three things Gavelling has no answer to at all: a countries × committees Country Matrix, native iOS/iPad/Android apps for live sessions, and eight languages against Gavelling's four. Against that, Gavelling is ahead on nearly every axis of depth: a scored, reason-tagged allocation suggestion engine, per-seat importance tiers and double-delegation modelling, a real invoice/payment ledger with manual-proof review, per-organiser section permissions, a live-status board across every committee, optimistic writes with genuine rollback, and a live-derived setup checklist that re-opens when state regresses rather than a static 4/4 counter. The single most important strategic difference is pricing and who pays: mymun charges the organiser €1 per user per day (roughly €3,100 for a 1,044-participant three-day conference) and ships a free session tier that explicitly does not persist state; Gavelling charges the organiser nothing, is merchant-of-record through their own Stripe Connect account, and its free sessions persist to Supabase with realtime sync. That is a structural advantage, not a discount — and it is currently invisible, because nothing anywhere in /manage says "Gavelling costs your conference nothing". Two things undercut the story badly: the Outfit and DM Mono typefaces are not loaded at all (removed in commit 91b40e8), so the entire visual identity currently renders in system-ui and Courier, and the chair page hard-blocks every mobile and tablet user-agent including iPad — the most common dais device at a real conference. Fix the font link and stop pretending the delegate-facing side of the product is desktop-only before building anything new.

## What mymun does well

**Work-queue design (applications / To Do)**
- One unified queue mixing individual chairs, whole delegations and individual delegates — the organiser's real task is "clear the inbox", not "process five role pages", so one scan path and one place the count goes to zero.
- Two segmented controls that *are* the workflow, not generic filters: "To accept | To assign | Any" and "Paid | Unpaid | Any" — application processing is genuinely decide-then-allocate crossed with one money axis, so encoding exactly that teaches the workflow with zero documentation.
- A red count badge on the To Do nav item ("39") — converts a pull workflow into a push one; queue depth is knowable from anywhere without visiting the page.
- Accept and Reject as two enormous outlined buttons, each roughly half the row width — Fitts's law at 991 repetitions; the difference between a decision every 2 seconds and every 5.
- Delegations as first-class rows carrying institution, city, country, "N Expected", "N Registered", "£X Paid", one Accept/Reject and a "Show Allocations and Members" expander — 74 delegations is 74 decisions, not 700.
- Empty states that name their own next action: "No preferences available. Use custom assignment to assign a position." plus the button — the rows with the least information are exactly where organisers stall.
- A density/columns toggle beside the filters — the same list is used for scanning hundreds and reading one.

**Money made ambient**
- Payment state inline on the application row, colour-coded and fractional: "0 / 1 Members Paid" (red), "1 / 1 Members Paid" (green), "Delegate Fee Paid" (green), "No fee required" (grey) — acceptance decisions are money-conditional, so this removes an entire Applications↔Financials cross-reference loop. The explicit grey "No fee required" matters as much as the red.
- Money first on the dashboard: two large tiles (income last week, total income) above everything — revenue-to-date is what organisers open the dashboard to find out.
- A crosstab statistics grid: rows per participant type (Delegates, Chairs, Faculty advisors, Observers, Delegations, expected/registered delegation members) against columns Last week | Total | Paid, closing with a bold "Total paid participants" — one table answers who, how fast and how many paid, and a table beats tiles when the reader wants to compare rows.
- Pricing legible in one sentence: €1 per user per day, free up to 10 users — a student treasurer can compute the line item before committing, which is a real conversion feature when spend needs society-committee approval.

**Allocation**
- A true countries × committees Country Matrix — allocation is bipartite but every allocation UI is committee-major; a country-major cut answers "is France seated anywhere?" and "which of my committees still has its P5 open?" in one scan.
- Three distinct cell states: filled-with-name / outlined-empty / blank — the outlined-vs-blank distinction is the whole product, because "this seat exists and nobody is in it" and "this country is not in this committee" look identical in every list UI, and conflating them is how a delegate gets promised a seat that was never on the roster.
- Per-column fill counters (0/39, 0/41, 0/45) in the column header — the summary and the evidence for the summary in one eye-line.
- Crisis-cabinet character positions as rows in the same grid as countries — one mental model, one search box, and crisis committees are never second-class.
- Country search, per-column filter, and an explicit always-visible "Reset Filters" — filtered grids are the classic "why is my data missing" trap.

**Conference lifecycle and IA**
- A donut allocation ring (43/45, 36/41, 32/39) on every card on the Committees index — "which committee is under-filled" is the most-asked question in the six weeks before a conference, answered on the page organisers already visit daily; a red/green arc makes the laggard findable in peripheral vision.
- Committee difficulty as a first-class field with the board physically grouped by it ("Level Beginner" as a heading) — a grouping header answers "where do the first-timers go" with no interaction, where a sort control only answers it after you press the right button.
- "Import" beside "+ Add committee" at equal visual weight — returning conferences rebuild the same 8–15 committees annually; offering the bulk path at the moment of the one-at-a-time path is 15 minutes versus 90.
- "Post Conference" as its own named sidebar section holding Reviews and Awards — naming the stage in the IA is what makes reviews and awards get done rather than quietly skipped, and reviews feed next year's discovery.
- Setup chunked into two named phases ("Page setup", "Application setup") over four numbered cards with completion fractions (4/4, 2/2, 1/1, 3/3) — the grouping tells you what *kind* of work is left, not just how much.
- Chair avatars on committee cards linking out to public mymun profiles — makes chair identity portable and gives chairs a reason to keep an account alive between conferences, at zero cost to the organiser.
- A contextual help card pinned in the sidebar that changes with the section ("HOW TO CREATE COMMITTEES") — just-in-time help beats a docs link for a product whose users turn over completely every year.

**Live sessions (MUN Command)**
- Native iOS, iPad and Android apps — the delegate role is a phone role (30–45 people per room, eight hours), and a native app removes the ~100px of Safari chrome, gets an app-switcher slot and holds state across backgrounding.
- "Works offline & real-time sync" as a headline claim — a committee room is the worst network environment in the building; a local-first store that reconciles on reconnect is the hardest thing to retrofit and the most defensible thing they claim.
- In-app delegate interaction named explicitly: voting, roll call, motions, moderated caucus — it moves work off the dais and stops the chair being a data-entry clerk, which is the largest single time saving available in a live session.
- A legible free/paid boundary (3 modes of debate and 3 motion types free; 11 modes and 21 motion types paid) — evaluable in one browser window with no signup, and the upgrade trigger is a concrete thing a chair will hit.
- Role-segmented marketing on /mun-command (Chair / Delegate / Organizer / Faculty Advisor tabs) — four roles buy for four different reasons.
- A separately-branded product identity ("MUN COMMAND — debate. better.") with its own page, pricing and named SG testimonial — the live tool becomes recommendable by name independent of the conference platform.
- One-click import from their conference platform into MUN Command — a switching cost dressed as a convenience; the two products defend each other.

**Visual craft**
- Flat white cards on light grey with 1px hairlines and small radii — near-optimal for a 991-row queue; depth is a scarce signal and they spend none of it on rows, all of it on the sidebar and the two decision buttons.
- Dark navy/teal sidebar with the active item filled solid — the strongest available figure/ground split between "where am I" and "what am I working on", needing no hover and no interpretation.
- Skeleton loaders matched to the real row shape (avatar circle plus four text bars) — stable layout from first paint, and the shape itself communicates what is coming and at what density.
- Conventionality as a strategy — Stripe/Linear-adjacent with no invented vocabulary, so an organiser handing the dashboard to a first-year committee member pays zero explanation cost. For a tool used a few weeks a year by a rotating volunteer team, familiarity beats memorability.

28. **Broaden reconnect catch-up beyond chat.** `useReSubscribeCatchUp` (`src/lib/useChatCatchUp.ts:70-80`) only calls `catchUpMessages`, which only refetches messages — after a socket drop the speakers list, delegate statuses, motions and documents stay silently stale until the next realtime event happens to fire, and the file's own docblock says realtime does not replay. Add a `getCommitteeByCode` merge on re-SUBSCRIBED, guarded by the `localUpdateTime` debounce for the acting chair and taken unconditionally on delegate/advisor which write nothing. A backgrounded delegate phone waking to a correct speakers list is worth more than a correct chat backlog. Effort: small.
29. **Replace the mobile hard block with a tiered gate, and warn earlier.** `MobileGate.tsx:12-14` blocks on user-agent alone — iPhone, iPad, Android, no width check, no orientation listener — wrapped around the entire chair render (`chair/[code]/page.tsx:2618, :3540`). iPad is the most common dais device, and the organiser live dashboard's own "join as Secretariat" action (`LiveModals.tsx:185-189`) opens `/chair/[code]` in a new tab, so a Secretariat member walking rooms with a tablet hits a wall. Worse, `/create` and `/join` are ungated and both push straight into `/chair/[code]` (`create/page.tsx:493`, `join/page.tsx:252, :287`), so a chair completes the whole setup flow and discovers the block at the finish line — and the gate's own line "(or just rotate your phone, we're not judging)" (`MobileGate.tsx:81`) is false, because there is no orientation or resize listener. Minimum: warn before the form on both pages, and fix or delete the rotate line. Then switch detection to `Math.min(innerWidth, innerHeight)`, keep the block for phones under 600px, and let tablets into a reduced dais layout reusing the existing `showSliders` / `showRollCall` toggles. Also note the delegate view is correctly ungated but renders inside FitToScreen with `BASE_H = 820` and no lower clamp, so on an iPhone with Safari chrome (innerHeight ≈ 553) the page draws at ~67% and 14px text lands at ~9 physical px. Effort: medium; the warning half is small and should ship immediately.
30. **Let delegates raise motions.** The delegate tab list is `session | documents | chat | stats` (`delegate/[code]/page.tsx:626, :1011-1016`) — `addPendingMotion` is imported at `:36` and never called, a dead import. mymun sells exactly this. Reuse the RaiseView form from `MotionsModal.tsx:363`, filtered to enabled types, behind a new `allowDelegateMotions` setting defaulted on; the chair side needs no change since motions already arrive over realtime. Note the form currently reads settings via `getSettings(code)` (`MotionsModal.tsx:363-364`), which returns `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` on the delegate page per AGENTS.md rule 14 — it must take motion config as props derived from `committee.dbSettings`. On a 40-delegate committee entertaining 15 motions, this removes ~15 typed forms from the dais. Effort: medium.
31. **Persist individual votes.** The entire roll-call tally lives in one React `useState` (`voting/[code]/page.tsx:414`, mutated at `:776` and `:785-790`); a refresh, crash or sleeping laptop mid-vote loses everything with no recovery, and the file writes no vote rows at all. Cheapest path with no schema change: one `logEvent` per cast (`committeeService.ts:890`) with type, docCode and choice, rehydrated on mount. That buys crash recovery, a permanent voting record for the committee report, and per-delegate voting data for export. Delegate-side voting should come after this, since it needs a durable row anyway. Effort: small for persistence, large for delegate voting.
32. **Give the Custom motion an optional effect, instead of chasing 21 motion types.** `PendingMotionType` is seven floor types (`types.ts:49`) and `custom` is a documented no-op (`types.ts:44-48`). But the chair can already extend a caucus (`chair/[code]/page.tsx:672, :935`), set speaker time (`:2426`) and add extra time (`:2241`) — those actions exist with no motion path to them. Add an optional `effect` field with three values — `extend-caucus(+Ns)`, `set-speaker-time(Ns)`, `close-debate` — wired to the handlers that already exist. A chair renames a Custom motion "Motion to Extend Debate Time" once and it both appears on the floor and does something. That converts the honest weakness into the honest strength. Effort: medium.
33. **Organiser announcements broadcast into live sessions.** No in-session broadcast path exists beyond a chair chat message to Everyone; `/manage/[slug]/communications` is email. Add a compose box on `/manage/[slug]/live` writing a `sender='__organizer__'` message into every selected committee's `messages` table, rendered as a pinned banner above the delegate Session tab and the chair header. Reuses the table, the subscription and the renderer, and replaces walking to twelve rooms to say "lunch is at one". Skip the conference schedule — it is a calendar, low differentiation, and organisers already run one. Effort: medium, no schema change.
34. **Finish the four locales before adding a fifth.** `translations.ts` has exactly four blocks with verified 853-key parity each (en `:4`, es `:860`, fr `:1715`, ar `:2570`), but several session components are hardcoded English: `FeedbackLogPanel.tsx`, `CowDelegationBoard.tsx` and `ChatDisabledNotice.tsx` have zero `t('` calls, `ScoreboardPanel.tsx` has two with literals at `:235, :237, :240`, and the whole organiser live dashboard is English. That is roughly 40 strings. Full parity in four beats eight half-done, and it is the honest counter-claim in a head-to-head. Effort: small for the three panels, large per additional locale.
35. **Smaller correctness and polish items.** The Delegations tile over-counts by taking distinct `society_id` across all applications including rejected and withdrawn (`manage/[slug]/page.tsx:943`) while Financials deliberately excludes them (`financials/shared.tsx:239`). The dashboard never refreshes — both effects key only on conference id and token (`page.tsx:855, :906`) — while Live Status beside it polls every 10s; poll at 60s with a manual refresh. The dashboard skeleton is terminal, with no try/catch and no error state (`:815-854, :909`). Invoices in a non-conference currency are silently excluded from every total (`shared.tsx:304-305`) while add-on and app-fee editors expose a full currency picker with no warning. The Financials Overview stat tiles are inert although `NeuStatTile` accepts `href` and `onClick` (`neu.tsx:306-307`) and both are used elsewhere. There is no bulk "Remind to pay" despite `runBulk` generalising over a handler. There is no Support route anywhere in `/manage`. The committees board inverts the house acronym rule — abbreviation as a 10px eyebrow above the full name at 14.5px with `minHeight: 2.4em` reserved for wrapping (`committees/page.tsx:1604-1616`, `:1432-1443`) — and never imports `committeeDisplayName`; while fixing it, promote assignment's `committeeLabels` into `presetNames.ts` so there is one implementation. Per-speech chair feedback is rendered only when `isViewOnly` (`chair/[code]/page.tsx:3332`), so a solo chair can set `scoreBlend` above 0 and get a headline score computed against permanently empty quality data. Unfilled crisis-character seats render as unlabelled grey `UserRound` discs with the name only in a `title` (`assignment/page.tsx:591-618, :2205-2211`), so a 20-character cabinet is 20 identical circles. `is_observer` is written on every slot but never selected into the assignment board (`:3019`), so an organiser cannot tell a non-voting seat from a voting one while allocating. Effort: each small.

## Where Gavelling is already ahead

- **The free tier persists.** mymun's free Session app states "Changes are not saved automatically" and runs in one browser window; Gavelling's `createCommittee` (`committeeService.ts:146-190`) writes real committees, delegates and current_speaker rows with no auth path, joined from any device over realtime. That is a category difference, not a feature gap — and it is currently not said out loud anywhere in marketing.
- **The organiser pays nothing.** Stripe Connect per conference with the organiser as merchant of record (`settings/page.tsx:117-147, :713-718`), against €1 per user per day. At 1,044 participants over three days that is roughly €3,100 to mymun and £0 to Gavelling — and the biggest, most prestigious conferences are the cheapest to win, exactly inverting mymun's incentive.
- **A conference committee *is* a live session — there is no import step.** `mintConferenceSession` (`CommitteeEditorModal.tsx:124-176`) inserts the session row with a generated code and chair suffix, seeds `current_speaker`, inserts one delegate row per roster country with observer flags preserved, and writes `session_id`/`session_code` back. mymun sells "one-click mymun import" precisely because their two products are separate systems. (Caveat: the mint triggered later from the committees page passes an empty roster at `committees/page.tsx:926`, so only the create-time path seeds delegates.)
- **A scored, ranked, explainable allocation engine.** `allocationScore` (`assignment/page.tsx:467`) blends preference-and-experience, slot and society fit; each suggestion carries reason chips and a colour-coded fit score, with swap detection reporting net gain across both delegates (`:3890-3985`) and one-click optimistic commit. Nothing observed in mymun resembles this.
- **Per-seat importance tiers and proper double-delegation modelling.** Importance is persisted on the slot and drives seat ordering, scoring and an open-seats-by-tier readout; `isSlotFull`, `lowestOpenSeat`, `siblingSeatAllocation` and `DelegationConflictModal` together stop a delegate being dropped into the second seat of a country held by a different delegation. mymun's matrix has one cell per country per committee and no seat dimension — every empty cell looks equally urgent, which is false: an open P5 seat and an open Vanuatu seat are not the same problem.
- **Delegations allocable as blocks**, dragged straight onto a committee, taking the whole country including both seats of a double slot (`SocietyDropAllocateModal`, `applyLocalSocietyAllocation`). For 74 delegations this is the single biggest time saver on the page.
- **Bulk CSV/XLSX allocation import** that resolves committee, country and seat, works identically for "France" and a crisis character, dedupes against existing allocations, and self-heals legacy applications carrying an assignment with no allocation row (`import/page.tsx:114-129, :205-258, :452-495`).
- **A real invoice and payment ledger**, not a status flag: separate `invoices`, `payments` and `payment_batches` tables with Transactions/Ledger/History views, plus manual-payment proof review with 300-second signed URLs and approve/reject with optimistic rollback (`invoices/page.tsx:424-479`) — essential given only 44 countries have Stripe Connect.
- **A hard payment-readiness gate**: applications cannot open and the conference cannot publish until delegates have somewhere to pay (`payments.ts:152-196`, banner at `settings/page.tsx:356-371`, publish block at `manage/[slug]/page.tsx:1089-1095`), including the free-conference case. This prevents the most expensive organiser mistake there is.
- **A far richer fee model**: phased fees, per-role fees, vouchers (percent and flat, currency-aware), an application fee, arbitrary add-ons targetable at seven audiences, and financial aid with partial grants surfaced in the review modal where the decision is made.
- **Payment provenance** — STRIPE / SELF-PAID / DELEGATION / MANUAL derived and shown per row (`shared.tsx:151-162`), answering the reconciliation question mymun's "1/1 Members Paid" raises but does not.
- **A live-derived setup checklist, not a completion counter.** Every row re-evaluates on load and a regression un-completes it and re-sorts it to the top (`manage/[slug]/page.tsx:965-1081`); sub-lines are diagnostic ("Only N seats for M expected delegates — K short"); each row deep-links to the exact fix, one branching into a share modal with a copyable link and a pre-written Instagram caption. mymun's cards read a static "4/4 steps complete".
- **Flat, always-visible navigation**: twelve destinations across five labelled groups, all rendered unconditionally (`layout.tsx:103-141`), with sub-navigation as in-page pills over real routes. mymun collapses Applications, Financials, Conference and Post Conference by default — which is where its own best feature, the Country Matrix, is buried.
- **Per-organiser section permissions** with nine keys, a real route-level gate and a named "SECTION RESTRICTED" state rather than a dead link (`layout.tsx:767-802`), plus token-based organiser invites through `create_organizer_invite`.
- **Live Status in the primary nav** — a 10-second-polled board of every committee's session (phase, current speaker, present count, queue, motions, documents, feedback) batched in one round trip, because Gavelling owns both halves of the product.
- **Applications-page decision quality**: MUN experience tier from the applicant's real CV count (batched into one query for all applicants), age resolved from profile or custom answer, nationality flag on the avatar, top three committee+country preferences with logos and flags, inline quick-allocate to a specific open seat with live counts and full committees greyed out, automatic queued emails on accept/reject with an inline notice when a template is switched off, per-row optimistic rollback, acceptance blocked-and-explained on unpaid gating invoices with the excluded count in the bulk label ("Accept 12 (3 unpaid)"), filter-safe bulk selection by construction, "Showing 43 of 991 — filters active" stated in words, rejection notes that become the rejection email, resubmission-aware "Awaiting Resubmission" instead of a final-sounding "Rejected", and one-click REINSTATE.
- **Wall-clock anchored session clocks** instead of per-second writes — `caucusRemainingNow`, `speakerRemainingNow`, `anchorCaucusClock` (`committeeService.ts:40-94`), with the reasoning documented: a per-second write is one write/sec/committee and re-arms the realtime debounce. The foundation is right even though the delegate side does not yet consume it.
- **Hard multi-chair correctness**: a claim-at-will gavel, a single-winner resume latch with compare-and-swap release and take-over, a self-heal for the chair who reloads mid-resume, and a 12-second take-over affordance. This is the class of bug that ruins a real conference and it has visibly been fought through.
- **Chat that survives what chat usually dies to**: a module-scope outbox that outlives unmount, catch-up on visibilitychange/online/focus/re-subscribe with monotonic union-by-id merge, and read state keyed per reader identity so two chairs on one dais laptop do not overwrite each other.
- **A configurable scoring ledger** with per-event attribution, nine built-in sources plus chair-added custom ones, CSV export of both the matrix and per-speech breakdowns with chair comments, and a delegate-facing view with a hide-scores switch. mymun sells "session statistics" as a paid feature.
- **Voting that models real procedure**: P5 veto, unanimous and custom veto-country modes, supermajority/consensus thresholds, quorum, an `abstentionsInDenominator` switch, and rights of explanation sequenced after the roll.
- **Renameable everything, chair-ordered**: all motions and both document types renameable, disruptiveness ranking drag-reorderable and re-read at sort time, plus a free-text Custom escape hatch — so one committee's rules of procedure need not match another's.
- **A guided in-product chair tutorial** with real spotlights bound to `data-tutorial` attributes on the live UI and action steps that auto-advance when the chair performs them, resolving the chair's own renamed document labels. mymun ships a floating "?" bubble.
- **Portaled, edge-flipped, scroll-repositioned popovers** as a codified house rule and an actual implementation — nothing in the product is clipped by a rounded card or a scrollable modal. Plus a toast system that never shifts layout and genuine `prefers-reduced-motion` handling.
- **Depth used as a semantic**: exactly three shadow pairs, extruded = live, pressed-in = done or inert, held consistently across checklist rows, active filter tiles, progress tracks and rings.
- **Richer status communication**: six gradient-filled pills with deliberately distinct glyphs (the four checkmark-family states get different ticks so they read apart at size), an orthogonal attendance badge, and a role pill.
- **The best-contrasting surface in either product** — the chair's forest rail measures 7.7–10.4:1 on every live value, and the whole row is tinted by status *and* is the control, so one gesture on a full-row target beats reading a chip and hunting a button.
- **Precisely targeted review prompting** — only for conferences that have actually concluded, deduped, excluding already-reviewed and dismissed — plus a share-achievement moment with a gold-foil card and native share sheet after a new CV entry.
- **Setup speed**: 13 committee presets and a bulk-paste roster parser splitting on newlines, commas, semicolons, tabs, bullets and sentence-ending periods, with a review modal.

## mymun's pitfalls to avoid

- **A free tier that does not persist state.** "Changes are not saved automatically" is a pricing-page footnote, and the failure lands mid-committee in front of forty delegates on a chair who reached the app through a link and never saw the pricing page. It converts by disappointment, which is the most expensive kind — the chair who lost a session tells the whole circuit. Never ship a tier whose failure mode is silent data loss.
- **Per-user-per-day pricing.** It scales against the customer (the more successful the conference, the more punishing the bill), lands the entire cost on the party with the least money and the shortest institutional memory, creates a perverse incentive to under-register or shorten the schedule, and introduces an adversarial accounting relationship into the one place the organiser most needs to trust the tool — the participant list. Gavelling's decoupling of roster from revenue is exactly why it can afford to make bulk-accept and import genuinely frictionless.
- **Gating basic procedural correctness behind the paid tier** (3 modes of debate and 3 motion types free). A committee needing an unmoderated caucus and a Consultation of the Whole is ordinary, not advanced. Crippling procedure teaches an evaluating chair that the free product is unusable rather than that the paid one is good, and hands a challenger the entire trial market.
- **Onboarding scaffolding that never retires.** Four numbered setup cards at full size and top-of-page even at 4/4, 2/2, 1/1, 3/3 — on a 1,044-participant conference the most valuable screen real estate tells the organiser about work finished months ago and pushes the numbers they came for below the fold. Gavelling has the same defect today; fix it rather than inherit it.
- **Collapsed-by-default sidebar accordions** on the sections containing the daily work. A new organiser cannot form a mental model of the tool, an experienced one pays a click on every navigation, and the Country Matrix — arguably their best feature — converts as if it did not exist because it is two levels down behind a closed accordion. Gavelling's flat nav is right; the temptation to add accordions will arrive around fourteen items and should be resisted in favour of tighter section headers. Watch that hover-to-expand does not become an equivalent hiding mechanism.
- **An unbounded loading state.** A large dashboard card that renders only a spinner and never resolves, with no timeout, error or empty state, sitting directly beneath the money figures. The user cannot distinguish loading from broken from empty, so they wait instead of acting — and an unresolved card next to financial numbers teaches them not to trust any number on the page. Gavelling has the same trap in three places today, and one of them is worse: a failed applications fetch renders "No applications yet", which is a confident false statement.
- **Emphasis inverted by recency rather than meaning.** "£0.00 income last week" in prominent amber against "£66,256.27 total income" in plain near-black. Colour is the strongest signal on a dashboard; spend it on state that needs action, not on whichever number is most recent, and never render a benign zero in a warning colour. Gavelling is not immune — the Financials Overview gives the *projected* total the loudest gold gradient while actually-collected gets green.
- **A floating orange "?" bubble pinned bottom-right, overlapping content.** It permanently occludes the corner where long lists put their last row — and on Gavelling that is exactly where the sticky bulk-action bar lives (`applications/page.tsx:2928`, `bottom: 20`). It also competes with their own contextual sidebar card, so the organiser must learn which help system answers which question. Gavelling's house rule already prefers hover-revealed inline hints; teaching in place beats floating over everything.
- **Reject given exactly the same target size as Accept, side by side, with no note captured.** The huge-target reasoning is right for Accept and wrong for Reject: the consequences are wildly asymmetric, a misclick is destructive, and the applicant gets no reason. Make Accept faster; do not make Reject as easy as Accept.
- **Full committee names inline and as grid headers.** "Full Committee Name - ACRONYM" on cards wrapping to two lines, and Country Matrix column headers wrapping to four or five, burning the most expensive band on a grid before a single data cell appears and making columns wider than they need to be. Gavelling has the rule written down and a helper for it — but the organiser committees board currently violates it and even reserves `minHeight: 2.4em` for the wrap. Fix that before building any grid view or the problem gets inherited.
- **A matrix whose rows are the full country universe.** With ~6 committees of ~40 seats, a 200-row grid is over 90% blank and the signal — outlined empty seats — is a sparse scatter across a void. Build rows from the union of countries that actually appear in at least one committee's slots, not from the UN list.
- **Twenty per-column filter icons** on a grid whose columns are all the same type. Hiding a committee is a column-visibility concern, not a filter; one global popover plus a column-visibility list does the same work with a fraction of the chrome.
- **The matrix living on a separate sidebar page from the queue where decisions are made** — the organiser context-switches between where they see the gap and where they fill it. Ship the grid as a mode on the assignment page sharing the same loaded data and the same assign handler, so a cell is both the evidence and the control.
- **Fill counters that can be wrong** — 0/39, 0/41, 0/45 on a conference with 1,044 paid participants. Whether that is an un-started allocation or a counter reading the wrong table cannot be told from outside, but either way: organisers make staffing and refund decisions off these numbers, so compute grid counters from the same helper the panels use rather than a second bespoke count.
- **Feature-count marketing** — "11 modes of debate", "21 motion types". Twenty-one items is a menu a chair scrolls while the room waits; the number optimises for a comparison table, not for the eight seconds between a placard going up and the room going quiet. The trap is deciding to match 21. On modes, Gavelling implements ten distinct debate states with their own UI and state machine — roll call, GSL, moderated caucus, unmoderated caucus, Consultation of the Whole, Tour de Table with three orderings, Right of Reply as an independent overlay with its own clock, the document reading→presentation→Q&A→vote flow, the voting procedure with sequenced rights, and suspend/adjourn. Ten against eleven is a rounding difference; do not concede it.
- **Awards and reviews as organiser-side records that end inside the platform.** An award that exists only in an admin panel creates no pull for the delegate. This is precisely where Gavelling can beat them rather than match them.
- **No dark mode.** Neither product has one, so this is parity — but committee rooms and crisis sessions run late in dim rooms and a full-white dais screen is loud on a projector, so a genuine low-light dais mode is a cheap visible differentiator that pairs naturally with the projector view.

## The three things I would do first

**1. Restore the fonts, then fix the frozen delegate clocks.** These are two separate items but they belong in one first pass because both are regressions against work already done, both are measured in minutes, and both are currently making the product look worse than it is. The entire distinctiveness argument — the Outfit display voice, the 900-weight stat numbers, the mono fill counters — is rendering in system-ui and Courier because a font link was removed in commit 91b40e8, and no other item on this list has that ratio of brand recovered to effort spent. Restore it with weights 800 and 900 that the old link never even requested, so the ~979 uses of those weights stop being faux-bolded. Then, in the same pass, add a 1s tick to the delegate and advisor pages and swap four render sites to `caucusRemainingNow` and one to `speakerRemainingNow`. Right now, during every unmoderated caucus, forty delegates watch a countdown that does not count down, and none of them can see how long the current speaker has left — and the correct, documented readers for both already exist and are used by the chair page. The delegate view is where thirty-plus people per committee actually judge this product; it should not be the half that looks broken.

**2. Build the queue-processing layer on the applications page.** Gavelling has the better-designed single row — richer decision context, safer optimistic writes with real rollback, filter-safe bulk selection, honest counts, automatic emails, inline allocate-to-a-specific-seat that mymun does not appear to have. What it lacks is everything that makes 991 decisions bearable: the always-visible `[To decide | To allocate | All]` × `[Paid | Unpaid | All]` segmented controls, the rail badge and awaiting-decision tile, keyboard operation with shift-click range select, a compact density mode, oversized Accept targets on submitted rows, and batched bulk writes. Batching `runBulk` is non-negotiable and belongs first inside this item: a bulk accept of 200 rows currently fires 200 `sync_conference_invoices` RPCs and 200 full-table refetches with four nested joins each, which is the one thing on this entire brief that can outright fail at LIMUN scale. Fix the two real bugs here at the same time — the CSV export that ignores filters and selection, and the missing error state that renders "No applications yet" when a fetch fails. mymun designed for 991 repetitions and Gavelling designed for one excellent decision; the queue layer is what closes the only area where they are meaningfully beating you on the screen that consumes the most organiser hours.

**3. Ship fill rings on the Committees index and a countries mode on the assignment page.** These are the two allocation gaps where mymun is genuinely ahead, and the first is nearly free. "Which committee is under-filled" is the most-asked question in the six weeks before a conference, and the page organisers visit daily answers it with capacity only — while the numerator, the ring component (used exactly once in the whole app), and the full/partial gradient rule all already exist one directory over; add one count query to the existing `Promise.all` and collapse the N+1 while you are in there. Then add the countries × committees mode beside the four existing assignment modes, using data `loadData` already fetches in a single query, with open cells wired straight into the existing assign modal so the grid is a second assignment surface rather than a report. Build it with the pitfalls designed out from the start: rows from the union of countries actually on a roster rather than the full UN list, acronym-plus-logo column headers instead of four wrapped lines, importance tier and delegation ownership marked on the cell, and double-delegation as two stacked mini-cells — all of which Gavelling already models and mymun does not. Done that way it is not parity with the Country Matrix; it is a strictly better version of their best feature, built on data that is already in memory.
