# How MUN crisis committees actually work: research for Gavelling

Compiled 25 Oct 2026. Sources marked **[P]** are primary documents I read in full (PDF text extracted); **[G]** are guide/blog pages fetched and summarised; **[M]** are modeldiplomat.com glossary pages, which read like generated SEO content: use them for vocabulary and as a "commonly claimed" signal, not as evidence of any conference's rule. Where I could not find evidence I say so.

Honest coverage gaps: I could not retrieve r/MUN threads (search surfaced none), could not find any source describing map or war-game adjudication in MUN specifically (only generic "backroom judges plausibility"), found no published backroom-to-delegate ratios, and found no published award weightings beyond qualitative statements. ChoMUN, HMUN, BMUN, SSUNS, McMUN, CMUNC, NHSMUN and WorldMUN crisis RoP PDFs were not found by search (several are behind JS pages or not indexed). Treat the numbers below as examples from the conferences named, not as industry standards.

## 1. Committee types

| Type | What it is | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Continuous / single-cabinet crisis | One room, one escalating storyline all weekend. Delegates are officials (ministers, generals, advisers), not countries. No set agenda. | VMUN [P], MUNUC [G], Best Delegate [G] |
| Cabinet | Subtype of the above: a government cabinet, often with the chair role-playing the head of state (e.g. the president). | MUNUC structure page [G], VMUN lists "Cabinet" as its own committee [P] |
| Joint crisis committee (JCC) | Two or more cabinets, usually opposed (rival governments, factions). Rooms do not meet directly; the backroom relays consequences between them. Some run three or more rooms (a neutral or insurgent third). | King's MUN [P], Best Delegate [G], WiseMee [G], [M] |
| Historical crisis (HCC) | Delegates play historical figures at a real moment, with divergence allowed. | King's MUN [P], IASAS/THIMUN manual [P], Best Delegate [G] |
| Fictional / fantasy / sci-fi | Invented setting (Star Wars, alternate history). | King's MUN lists Fictional, Concept, Historical [P]; Acera MUN had a Star Wars crisis and an alternate-history 1933 Russia crisis [G]; WiseMee lists contemporary-UN, contemporary non-UN, historical, futuristic, fantasy/sci-fi [G] |
| Ad hoc | Crisis committee whose topic and positions are revealed only when the first session starts; tests fast research and directive drafting. | Best Delegate, NAIMUN ad hoc page, [M] (search snippets only) |
| Hybrid | A crisis-style room combined with another format (e.g. historical plus futuristic branching). Only seen in [M]; treat as unverified. | [M] |
| Short "unspecified crisis" afternoon committee | Two-hour introductory crisis, speakers list kept, directives announced aloud not typed. | IAC Regional MUN [P] |

Disagreement: VMUN and NAIMUN say crisis has no speakers list; IAC's beginner crisis keeps a speakers list (default 60 s) and uses random draw to seed it.

## 2. Roles and what they do minute to minute

**Crisis Director (CD).** Runs the backroom, writes the crisis breaks, synthesises the front room's actions with each delegate's private arc, tracks arcs, and steers the weekend (MUNUC [G]). At MUNUC the CD "usually doesn't answer notes, though sometimes does" (MUNUC). WiseMee: CD has final say on direction, pacing and tone. Best Delegate's crisis-room guide: the CD can dictate notes to staffers rather than write them, and an under-secretary-general coordinates pacing when committees are integrated. VMUN: the Director is final authority on the RoP and can suspend or change them, interrupt debate to show a presentation or bring in a guest speaker or expert witness.

**Assistant Chairs / crisis staff / backroomers.** MUNUC: several ACs per committee; they answer notes, act out updates in character, gather information from the front room, consult the CD, and report back. Staffers also play "un-simulated" bodies (outside contacts); Best Delegate says assign the same staffer to each outside contact for continuity. WiseMee splits further: Deputy CDs, Backroom Directors, a **Cabinet Director** per cabinet (approves small requests, escalates big ones to the CD), backroom staff who read and implement directives, and **NPCs** played by staff. No source gave a ratio. Acera MUN (small school event, 21 delegates, two crisis committees) had a "crisis backroom team" of about four people and reported the backroom was "a little overwhelmed" by many assassination attempts.

**Front-room chair / dais.** Opens and closes sessions, recognises speakers, rules on points, enforces rules, can rule a motion dilatory (VMUN). MUNUC: also plays an in-character role and has final say on directives. WiseMee: leads the cabinet and communicates with the backroom. At IASAS/THIMUN the chair must approve a public directive before the motion to introduce is raised. Some committees add a vice chair or rapporteur; no crisis source I read described a rapporteur's duties.

**Runners / pages.** Carry paper notes between delegates, dais and backroom; must keep notes confidential; in some crisis rooms the dais screens notes first. HMUN 2023 historical crisis runners reportedly carried hundreds of private directives per session ([M] runner page; single unverified claim). King's MUN: give notes to the page, who passes them on; the note must say who it is from and to, otherwise the page cannot deliver it.

**Delegates.** Play a character with a portfolio. Two jobs: front room (debate, write and pass directives) and backroom (crisis notepad: gather resources, run covert actions, gain influence) (Illinois MUNI [G]).

**JCC inter-cabinet coordinators.** No source describes a named role. Best Delegate's guide describes CDs of each cabinet coordinating, and warns that poor CD-to-CD communication breaks integration. Rooms communicate through communiqués, envoys, joint sessions, or backroom-relayed press releases ([M], Middlebury [P] says a press release can be a way to talk to another committee).

## 3. Flow of a session

Typical loop (Illinois MUNI [G], NAIMUN [P], King's MUN [P]):

1. Crisis break / update arrives.
2. A few moderated caucuses, often opening with a round robin (one short speech each, usually 30 s to 1 min).
3. Unmoderated caucus, around 10 minutes, mostly to write and merge directives (not bloc building).
4. Directives presented and voted.
5. Passed directives are sent to the CD; consequences come back in the next update. Repeat.

Details by conference:
- **NAIMUN [P]:** No speakers list. Moderated caucus is the default if nothing else is on the floor; chair rarely allows more than 10 to 12 minutes; with so few delegates one person can speak twice in a caucus. Unmods rarely exceed 10 min. Round robins at most about 1 min per speaker. Roll call, no motion to open debate, no seconds needed. A "timed crisis" lets the chair relax procedure and allow a directive to be presented verbally. Trial procedure exists (guilty needs 2/3; penalty is loss of speaking or voting privileges).
- **VMUN [P]:** Default format is a *continuous* moderated caucus at 1 min per speaker, no limit on number of speeches. Directives may be presented only after every fifth speaker, and only on topics debated by at least five speakers just before. Updates are delivered as **slideshows made by the Director**, with optional guest speakers and Q&A.
- **IAC [P]:** Briefing paper read for 3 minutes, opening remarks 30 s, speakers list default 60 s (30 to 90 s), moderated caucus default 45 s, unmod 5 to 15 min. After a directive passes the chair tells the CD and "typically this news arrives anywhere from 2 to 10 minutes." Delegates may leave seats to draft directives. News goes to all delegates at once. Voting can be interrupted by new information.
- **Delivery formats for updates** (Best Delegate crisis-room guide [G]): news-channel segments, newspapers, pamphlets, pre-recorded video, Skype call-ins, secret notes; stickers with a body's emblem on secret letters; guest speakers (VMUN, IAC).
- **In-world time:** the backroom controls it; several real hours can be days or weeks in-world; a crisis usually spans under a year (WiseMee [G]). Staff are the source of truth: if staff did not announce it, it did not happen.
- **Crisis cadence:** [M] claims conferences aim for an update every 15 to 30 minutes; I found no primary document stating this. IAC's primary text says 2 to 10 minutes after a directive.

## 4. Directives in detail

### Taxonomy (vocabulary differs by conference)

| Term | Meaning | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Public / committee / cabinet directive | Written by a group, introduced, debated, voted. Everyone sees it. | VMUN, King's, NAIMUN, MiddMUNC, IASAS |
| Private / personal directive / crisis note | One delegate to the backroom, secret, no vote. Judged by staff. | VMUN ("private"), MiddMUNC ("crisis note, also known as a personal directive"), King's ("private directive") |
| Joint private directive / joint person directive (JPD) | Two or more delegates co-sign a private action. Best Delegate warns co-authors can exploit loopholes by sending extra notes. | King's, Best Delegate, WiseMee |
| Communiqué | Committee message to a foreign government or body; negotiation, threat, aid request. | VMUN (as a type of external communication), SAMUN [G], [M] |
| Press release | Public statement to sway opinion; can be misinformation. A group press release needs sponsors/signatories and a vote like a directive. | MiddMUNC [P], SAMUN, King's |
| Portfolio request | SAMUN's name for a unilateral individual action limited to real powers. | SAMUN [G] |
| Covert vs overt | IASAS: *covert* = passed secretly; *overt* = author stays undisclosed but public effects are announced to the committee. VMUN: private directives are not shared unless specified. | IASAS [P], VMUN [P] |

VMUN's public directive scope list (useful as a type taxonomy for a tool): enforcement action, political, internal communication, external communication (communiqués, public service announcements, invitations and requests), military (operation orders, sorties), other.

WiseMee [G] type list: strategic, military, personal, intelligence, diplomatic, economic, press release.

### Fields and format
- **VMUN [P]:** every directive must have a **title**, a **public/private flag**, **delegate signature(s)** and **actionable content**. Public ones list Sponsors and Signatories separately. Length: "a few lines to several sentences."
- **King's MUN [P]:** short title; sponsors and signatories; optional preamble (chair decides); numbered action clauses beginning with a verb; be specific (units, place, duration). Private note: handwritten, "To Back Room", "From [character]".
- **MiddMUNC [P]:** a good crisis note is (1) addressed to a named *character* (not "Crisis"), (2) uses the delegate's own resources, (3) states direct specific actions, (4) states how they will be done. Directive: title, falls within the committee's purview, specific actions, clause format with sub-clauses allowed, Sponsors and Signatories listed.
- **IASAS/THIMUN [P]:** private directive = submitter, recipient (committee, historical person, country), content. Public = main submitter, optional co-submitters, sponsors, content.
- **WiseMee [G]:** goal, how it will be implemented, costs, duration, and a clear path to an outcome; follow any rate limit the staff set on directive frequency.
- **MUNUC [G]:** a note covers what, when, why, how. Long notes give the backroom material to exploit.
- **Best Delegate [G]:** short action bullets; if long, add a summary at the end.
- Notepads: Illinois MUNI uses a **two-pad system**, each pad addressed to one contact, contacts cannot be other committee members (those pads are rejected; sticky notes are for collaboration). MUNUC gives one or two pads at the start. Notes numbered so the backroom can order arcs (search snippet, modeldiplomat).

### Sponsors, signatories, thresholds

| Conference | Needed to reach the dais | Vote | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| VMUN [P] | 3 signatures; generally also 2 sponsors (dais discretion) | Simple majority, dais holds a substantive veto; **consensus** needed for nuclear weapons or ultimatums | Presented only after every 5th speaker; a directive of up to 2 lines may be oral; private directives need no co-signers and are passed at dais discretion |
| IAC [P] | at least 2 co-sponsors; signatories optional | Simple majority, abstentions ignored, ties fail | 1 for / 1 against, 30 s each; friendly amendments only; typically half a page; announced orally |
| IASAS/THIMUN [P] | at least one third of the committee as sponsors including submitters; below that, auto-denied | clause-by-clause style debate then vote | Chair must pre-approve before the motion; only main submitter moves it; reading aloud clause by clause; POCs/POIs accepted; moderated (30 s) or unmod caucus first; private directives passed or failed by the chairs alone; assassination of ministers only by chairs |
| NAIMUN [P] | "required number of signatories" set by dais | Majority; 2 for / 2 against speeches; no speeches against means automatic pass; no roll-call votes; no Q&A | Friendly amendment = verbal agreement of all sponsors; unfriendly needs a vote; divide the question needs a majority; dais may cap number of directives, forcing merges |
| King's MUN [P] | numbers set by committee size, announced after roll call | raise placards; optional Q&A (e.g. 2 min) | |
| Illinois MUNI [G] | dais-set minimums; may submit without merging | majority; 2 for / 2 against or 1/1 in small rooms | dais might accept only 2 or 3 directives, max 3 pages each |

Do directives need chair approval first? IASAS: yes, explicitly. VMUN: the Dais "must approve all decisions" and holds a veto. King's: sponsors present, then Q&A, amendments, vote. Others imply the chair gatekeeps via the cap on how many reach the floor.

## 5. How the backroom answers a personal directive

- **Outcome types:** confirmation, partial success (goes ahead with complications such as leaks or diplomatic fallout), failure (blocked, exposed or backfires, sometimes a public update), request for clarification ([M] backroom-response). VMUN says staff can pass, partially pass or reject. A mid-tier conference (an unnamed rules page via search) says incomplete directives are scrapped and quality, detail and reasonableness decide pass, partial pass or reject. Illinois: you must also write public clauses that cover problems your own covert action created.
- **Specificity rewarded:** detailed notes tend to be granted, vague ones rejected (MUNUC). Backrooms deliberately look for holes in shady notes (MUNUC, Best Delegate). Always reply, even just to confirm a note was read; reply proportionately (not one line to a detailed war plan); answers from different staffers must be consistent (Best Delegate crisis-room guide).
- **Turnaround:** primary documents give none for notes. IAC: 2 to 10 minutes for a directive consequence. Best Delegate: answer everything by end of session or say a longer answer comes next session. [M]: 15 to 30 minutes per note as an aim (unverified).
- **Queue fairness:** not described in any primary source beyond "don't reward only loud delegates" ([M]) and Best Delegate's warning against railroading. Real conferences presumably triage informally; this is an open pain point and design opportunity.
- **Arc tracking:** MUNUC: the CD tracks every delegate's arc and each weekend arc has three phases: build resources, build logistics, execute. Best Delegate: day 1 establish a foundation, middle sessions build to a small peak the staff bring into the room, day before the end execute the climax. Delegates may write dozens of notes over a weekend. Staff keep a note log and check for contradictions. Good notes can earn a crisis break built around that delegate.
- **Resources/assets:** no source has a formal ledger; notes are expected to use the character's own resources. Best Delegate says to "keep notes organized, don't lose them, avoid contradictions."
- **Death, arrest, removal:** WiseMee: character death, firing or being voted out gives the delegate a *new character*, and in a JCC possibly resurrected in the other committee; defection between cabinets is allowed keeping the character's personality. Best Delegate: an assassinated delegate returns as a new character, possibly better placed; assassination approval is the chair/CD's call and works better after multiple sessions of setup. IASAS: ministers can only be assassinated by the chairs. MUNUC forbids drugs, unnecessary violence or killing, war crimes; a discovered wrongdoer can be put on trial and lose progress. NAIMUN: trial needs 2/3 to convict; penalty is censure. Acera MUN: many assassination attempts overwhelmed their backroom.

## 6. Delegate to delegate communication
- Paper notes via pages in the front room (King's, IAC examples: yield requests, "meet me in the back"). Illinois forbids pads to other committee members; sticky notes only. Notes are unopened by others.
- Unmoderated caucuses for face-to-face meetings; IAC lets delegates leave seats at any time to draft.
- In-world meetings between characters may be requested via directives ("invitations and requests", VMUN) and "monitored by the chairs" (IASAS).
- Cross-committee contact in a JCC goes only through staff-monitored channels: communiqués, press releases, envoys ([M], Middlebury, SAMUN lists contacting delegates in other crisis committees as a portfolio action).
- No electronics at all in VMUN, IAC, Gettysburg; so every in-session communication is paper or voice.

## 7. Military, maps, territory
- Military action is mostly textual: VMUN requires an *operation order* (detailed method plus objectives) for multi-unit operations and *sorties* (aircraft or ships from a base, with a mission); King's example: "send 6000 soldiers to X for Y time"; IASAS requires numbers, source and location of troops and says severe military action produces consequences injected by chairs; war declarations need chair approval of a justified cause; SAMUN example: "arm 50% of missiles within 100 miles… scramble 35 B-2s".
- The backroom adjudicates plausibility and consequence; **I found no source describing a shared map, fog of war or movement rules in MUN.** Best Delegate says decide in advance what is forbidden (nuclear weapons, time travel). [M] notes information asymmetry in JCCs makes intelligence directives valuable. Everything map-related in the Gavelling plan is therefore a design choice, not a documented convention.

## 8. Portfolio powers
- Starting capabilities and resources from the bio; delegates are expected to infer reasonable powers from the real role (Best Delegate, [M]).
- Constraint: the single most common reason a note is denied is exceeding powers (justice minister ordering airstrikes; US Attorney General cannot mobilise troops: MiddMUNC). Staff only accept actions "within the purview" of the character; committee directives must be in the committee's purview, though MiddMUNC lets a body work with other government bodies with crisis-room approval.
- Interpol MMUN guide (snippet): use portfolio-power questions only for information not available elsewhere. VMUN: ministers may send brief queries to crisis staff about their own responsibilities; longer queries go through public directives.
- Joint notes pool powers but are riskier.

## 9. Awards
- No primary source gave a weighting. Qualitative: awards favour delegates who balance crisis notes, directives and speeches (Best Delegate); "disastrous rogue arcs" hurt; crisis staff "have a say" in awards; WiseMee says awards rely mainly on directive outcomes, then front room performance judged by the chair; [M] claims note quality counts heavily at many circuits. MUNUC links an awards PDF I could not read.
- Design implication: the tool should expose per-delegate backroom outcomes (notes sent, resolved, impact) and front-room participation (speeches, directives sponsored) side by side, because both are named inputs and neither is quantified anywhere.

## 10. Pain points
Direct delegate/staff complaints from r/MUN were **not retrievable**. What the sources explicitly say or imply:
- **Overloaded backroom → stalled updates and unanswered directives**, called the biggest failure mode in [M] (claim, not primary). Acera MUN backroom "overwhelmed". VMUN itself warns that too many private directives overwhelm the dais and asks delegates to merge similar ones.
- **Railroading** and **disproportionate replies** (Best Delegate, [M]).
- **Inconsistent answers** from different staffers; **contradictions in the note log**; **notes lost** (Best Delegate says "don't lose them").
- **Information inequality in JCCs**: one committee getting crises earlier than the other; weak CD-to-CD communication (Best Delegate).
- **Unclear rules** about which technology, information or meetings are allowed (Best Delegate).
- **Volume of paper**: hundreds of private directives per session at a big historical crisis (single unverified claim), runners as a bottleneck and a confidentiality risk.
- **Time lapse** after a note slows the whole committee (Best Delegate).
- **Opaque favouritism** feelings for delegates (the [M] claim; delegates cannot see their queue position).
- **Rules that depend on the chair**: pass thresholds, caps on directives, sponsor numbers are all dais discretion, so delegates cannot predict them (VMUN, NAIMUN, IASAS).
- **No digital tooling documented.** Sources describe paper/pad systems; nothing on Google Forms, Discord or spreadsheets. Whether those workflows are painful should be validated by interviewing CDs directly.

| Item | Values seen |

| Moderated caucus speaking time | 30 s to 1 min typical; VMUN default 60 s; IAC default 45 s (30 to 90) |
