How a Crisis Committee Works

From six real crisis procedure documents (NAIMUN, VMUN 2026, King's MUN, MiddMUNC, IAC, IASAS) and the main guides (Best Delegate, MUNUC, Illinois, WiseMee, SAMUN). Sources are listed at the end of this section.

Committee types

  • Single crisis or cabinet: one room, one storyline all weekend. Delegates play people (ministers, generals), not countries. In a cabinet the chair often plays the head of state.
  • Joint crisis committee (JCC): two or more cabinets, usually enemies, in separate rooms. They never meet directly; the backroom carries consequences between them. Some add a third neutral room.
  • Historical, fictional, futuristic: same mechanics with a different world. Fictional maps matter here.
  • Ad hoc: the topic and characters are revealed at the first session.

The loop

Crisis break→Moderated caucuses→Unmod to write and merge→Directives presented and voted→Backroom works out consequences→Next crisis break

Personal directives run all the time underneath this loop. Updates arrive as slides, news segments, video, guest speakers or secret notes. In-world time is controlled by the backroom: a few real hours can be days or weeks.

Numbers that recur

  • Moderated caucus speeches 30 to 60 seconds; caucuses rarely over 10 to 12 minutes; unmods about 10 minutes.
  • Directives are 2 or 3 clauses, up to half a page. Personal notes "a few lines to several sentences".
  • A passed directive's consequence comes back in 2 to 10 minutes (IAC). No primary source gives a reply time for personal notes.
  • Arcs have three phases: build resources, build logistics, execute. Delegates write dozens of notes over a weekend.
  • No published staff ratio. Maximun's own guide suggests one backroomer per 3 to 5 delegates.

The pain points we are solving

  • Overloaded backroom: notes go unanswered and updates stall. Delegates cannot see that they are waiting in a queue at all.
  • Different staffers give inconsistent answers; the note log contradicts itself; notes are lost.
  • Replies that do not match the effort (one line back for a full war plan), and railroading.
  • In a JCC, one room gets news before the other.
  • Rules depend on the chair, so delegates cannot predict them.

Gaps to be honest about: no Reddit complaints were retrievable, no conference publishes award weightings, and no source describes how maps, fog of war or troop movement are adjudicated. Military action in MUN is text today.

The Competition

Master of Disaster was explored hands-on through its public demo, Maximun through your signed-in test committee in Chrome (looking only, nothing saved), and both through their public sites.

Master of Disaster

masterofdisaster.co.uk, by one developer (Jonas Bokelmann). Five separate apps (USG, Director, Backroom, Chair, Delegate), server-rendered, every action reloads the page. Licensed per conference with participant quotas. The European default since Deus (UNA-York) shut down.

Does well

  • Directive timer (210 s between directives per delegate) and hourly message credits ("carrier pigeons")
  • Chair approves cabinet directives; chair's notes feed awards
  • Characters have location, resources and military units set by the backroom; alive or dead, kill with a cause, a Cemetery page
  • Visibility switch for other cabinets' characters: hidden, names only, full
  • "Make into news release" in one press; live news ticker
  • Battles: two commanders, co-belligerents, secret unit types, both plans revealed together
  • Backroom stats: replies and word counts per staffer

Does badly

  • Looks like 2016 Bootstrap, pure blue buttons, joke themes
  • The queue is not a queue: no waiting time, no oldest first, 15 big cards per page across 16 pages
  • A reply is just the first comment in a thread: no outcome, no templates, no internal notes
  • Replies cannot change the world; the director edits resources on another page
  • Message approval is a dropdown per message across 446 pages
  • Passwords made by hand and shown in plain text
  • No map, no voting, no phone-first delegate view

Maximun

maximun.app. One polished single-page app with Google sign-in, a teal sidebar, serif headings and 18 colour themes. Makers, pricing and customers are not public.

Does well

  • Round, "submissions open or paused" and the in-world clock in the top bar for every role
  • Crisis clock that advances itself (every 30 real minutes = 1 crisis month)
  • Directive pipeline: unclaimed, claimed, waiting for delegate, delegate overdue, "result not published" so results can be held and released together
  • Clarification follow-up: the chair is prompted to remind slow delegates
  • Resource Index: a global price chart (oil) plus each delegate's holdings, every change needs a reason
  • "AI Backroomer (beta)" reviewer and an AI-readable library of crisis guides
  • "Nothing is published until you confirm" on every form

Does badly

  • No voting, speakers list, motions or meetings: the front room is missing
  • Characters are a free-text name: no bio, powers, goals or picture
  • 10 staff and 30 delegate slots, each edited one at a time; no import
  • Two 32-character join codes for everyone; no per-person invite, no QR
  • One URL for the whole app: no links to a directive, back button does nothing
  • Map: real world only, fictional "coming later"; no sign of units or fog of war
  • Messages are one-to-one only; audit log has no dates; exports are TXT only

Not seen in Maximun: a filled directive, the reply form, the delegate and chair views, and the map editor. The agent stopped before "Open map editor" because the audit log shows it writes your test committee's map settings. Question 39 asks if I may press it.

Other ways conferences run crisis today

  • Delegate phone, seat claims, chat, Secretariat broadcasts
  • Realtime sync, offline-safe writes, database clock

Conference layer

  • Committee seats can be characters, not countries
  • Applications, allocation, imports, payments, emails

Around the room

  • Organiser live wall with broadcasts (pause, end)
  • Press Desk with press releases into rooms

The rule this keeps: a crisis committee is still one conference committee with one live room, minted by the same single path. The crisis layer adds tables and pages around it. It never writes the room's settings blob, phase or clocks behind the chair's back, so nothing in AGENTS.md changes.

Life of a personal directive

Draft→Sent→With a backroomer→Asked for detail→Answered→Held for the round→Released

Outcomes: approved, partly works, denied, ask for more detail, answer later. The delegate sees the reply and, if you want, the outcome label (question 14).

Drafted with sponsors→Chair approves (setting)→Introduced on the floor→Voted on the voting page→CD applies it→Consequences in the next break

The fair queue

Default order: longest waiting first, ties broken by who heard back least recently. Each row also shows length (reading time) and whose delegate it is, so nobody takes another backroomer's arc by mistake. A delegate never sees other people's directives, but can see "3rd in line" (question 15).

The War Map

The plan is "as good as a war game" without becoming one. The backroom stays the referee: the map shows the world, it does not resolve battles on its own (unless you want dice, question 30).

Building blocks

  • Regions, not freehand lines. A map is made of regions (countries, or provinces when we need finer borders). Each region has an owner. A border is just where owners change, so "click a country and expand the border" is one press per region, and borders are always clean. The design shows this with the Borders tool.
  • Three ways to start a map: the real world today (Natural Earth data, free), a historical preset (Europe 1914, 1939, the Cold War, borders set for that year), or your own image (a fantasy map or a scanned historical map) cut into regions in the editor.
  • Units: preset types (army, navy, air, missile, base, spy, submarine) and custom ones with a name and icon. Strength, owner and notes per unit.
  • Fog of war per side: a side sees its own regions, its own units and a radius around them, plus anything the backroom reveals or marks as "suspected". The CD can view the map as any side.
  • Lines and areas: blockades, fronts, supply routes, no-fly zones.
  • Replay: every change is stored with who and when, so the CD can scrub back and the conference gets a timelapse at the end.
  • Linked to directives: a reply can move units, change owners or reveal something, and the change is logged against that directive.

How it would be built

  • MapLibre GL (open source, fast on phones) for the real-world and historical maps, a flat picture mode for image maps.
  • Regions and units in the database with row-level security, so a delegate's phone only ever receives what their side may see. Fog of war enforced in the database, not just hidden in the browser.
  • Realtime updates through the same Supabase channel pattern as the live rooms.

Testing in a Real Room

You asked that every update is checked in a real room so nothing overlaps and every directive arrives. The plan:

  • A permanent test crisis on a test conference: one JCC with two rooms, a CD, two backroomers, two chairs and six delegates.
  • A scripted run after every change with real browsers side by side (CD, backroomer, chair, two delegate phones): send a directive from each phone, check it lands in the right queue within seconds, answer it, check the reply and any map or resource change reaches only the right side, pass a cabinet directive through the voting page, send a crisis break, pause directives, kill and replace a character.
  • The existing room must still work: the same run checks GSL, caucus, motions and voting on the chair console, so crisis work never breaks Sessions.
  • Layout: every page at 1280, 1440 and 1920 wide and on a phone, checked for overlaps and clipped text.
  • Short report per run: what passed, what failed, what I could not check.

Needs your sign-off: CLAUDE.md says runs are tested with a build and lint only, no browser and no test data, and that database objects are changed only through the chat's Supabase work. This plan needs both rules relaxed for crisis work. Questions 37 and 38.
