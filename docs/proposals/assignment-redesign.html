Seat Assignment, Three Ways Forward

Three redesigns of /manage/<slug>/assignment for conferences with up to 20 committees, individual applicants and delegations alike. Each keeps the suggested assignments engine and the click a card, then Assign flow exactly as they are today.

5,500

lines in today's page.tsx, with the board, rail, modals and suggestions in one file

20

committees to design for, stacked today in a masonry of panels the organiser scrolls through

5

modes on one URL today: Delegates, Chairs, Delegations, Independents, Give seats

What works today

  • Suggested assignments: a ranked strip of up to six picks with a fit score, reason chips and a swap pass that improves the total. Organisers trust it.
  • Click a card, then Assign: one press seats a suggestion, with an optimistic board and a reconcile that never loses a write.
  • The drop pop-up after choosing a committee lists open seats by importance, then by fit for that person.
  • Allocation emails: automatic or manual release, a Send menu with All new, Everyone and Choose individually.

What fails today

  • Scrolling: every committee is drawn at once. At 20 committees the organiser scrolls past rooms they are not filling to reach the one they are.
  • Drag: the rail scrolled away and HTML5 drag barely scrolled the page, so long drags failed (fixed in this run with pointer drag and a sticky rail, but the page shape still fights it).
  • Delegations: block seats live in a separate rail toggle, then a separate Delegations mode, then Give seats. Three places for one job: how many seats each delegation gets, and where.
  • No view of one committee's countries as a list with who sits where and who should.

20 committees

Only one is drawn in full. The index is 20 short rows with a fill bar, so the emptiest room is one glance away; clicking it never scrolls the page. Keyboard: up and down move through committees.

Individuals and delegations

One list per committee shows both: a person's seat, or a delegation's block seat with its name. The Delegations tab in the rail lists delegations with seats still to hand out, and dropping one on an open country gives it the block seat.

Suggestions, drag and click

The same engine, scoped to the open seats on screen: each open row carries its best pick. Assign stays one press; drag from the rail onto a row works as a second way, and the row is a large target, not a small flag.

Why it works

  • Matches how organisers think: fill DISEC, then ECOSOC.
  • Drop targets are full rows, so drag is easy even on a trackpad.
  • The page is short at any committee count.

Trade-offs

  • Loses the whole-board glance of today; the index bars have to carry it.
  • Moving someone between two committees takes two views (or the row menu's Move to).
  • The global suggestion strip becomes per committee; the cross-committee swap pass needs a home (Assign all suggestions on the index).

20 committees

Committees are 92px tiles, 20 of them fit in two or three rows. Nothing scrolls while seating; a tile opens its committee list when the organiser wants to review a room.

Individuals and delegations

Next delegation runs the same queue for delegations: how many seats they asked for, then the best open countries for a block, assigned one country per key press.

Suggestions, drag and click

The suggestion engine becomes the whole page: each person's top three seats. Click or a number key assigns; drag is only for the exception (drop on a tile, pick the country).

Why it works

  • Fastest for big first passes: 200 delegates is 200 key presses with a check each time.
  • Every person gets looked at once, so nobody is forgotten at the bottom of a rail.
  • Works on a phone: one card and three buttons.

Trade-offs

  • Weak at review and rebalancing: seeing who sits in ECOSOC needs another view.
  • Speed is also risk: with automatic emails on, a run of presses emails many people (the first-allocation pop-up and the 3-minute buffer matter here).
  • Greedy, person by person: the global swap pass is lost unless the queue order is the engine's order.

20 committees

Folded, 20 rows fit one screen. The organiser opens the two or three rooms they are working on; open rows remember themselves. Drop on a folded row to choose the country in the pop-up, or on an open seat inside an opened row.

Individuals and delegations

The plan answers the delegation question first (how many seats, in which committees, kept fair with the existing concentration and spread rules), then the seats step places countries. One place instead of three.

Suggestions, drag and click

The suggestion strip stays exactly where it is above the board. Click then Assign unchanged; drag works on folded rows and open seats with the new pointer drag.

Why it works

  • Smallest change: organisers who know today's page keep their habits.
  • The delegation plan is a real fix for the hardest part of the job.
  • Reuses today's panels, so it can ship in steps.

Trade-offs

  • Still a long page when many rows are open.
  • Two steps for delegations is one more concept to teach.
  • Does not change the person-by-person pace of a first pass.
Recommendation

Build Committee Focus, with the delegation plan from proposal 3

Committee Focus fixes the two complaints at their root: the page stops growing with the number of committees, and every drop target becomes a full row. Organisers already fill one room at a time; the index makes that the shape of the page, and its fill bars keep the whole-board glance. Suggestions move next to the seat they fill, which is easier to trust than a strip far above the room.

Take the delegation plan from proposal 3 as the Delegations tab: decide how many seats each delegation gets per committee first, then place countries in the same committee list. Keep proposal 2's number keys as a shortcut inside a committee (1 assigns the first open row's suggestion), so a fast first pass is still possible without a separate mode.

Order of work: split page.tsx into the index, the committee list and the rail (new files, mounted by a thin page), then move suggestions per seat, then the delegation plan. The pointer drag, the sticky rail and the allocation email buffer from this run carry over unchanged.
